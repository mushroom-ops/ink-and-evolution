The Culture of Tattoos: Identity,
Art, and Society

Humans have decorated their bodies for religious and cultural purposes for over thousands of years. From ancient rituals to modern self-expression, tattoos are a part of many cultures around the world.

Deep Roots in Oceania and Maori
Traditions

While the earliest recorded tattoo is from ancient Egypt , the practice holds incredibly deep cultural roots in the Pacific, dating back to the initial migration and colonization of the region.

  • The Body as a Canvas: Oceania is a warm climate place where people wear few clothing and consider themselves blank canvases for decoration.
  • Celebration of Self: Tattooing is highly celebrated in their culture and is very important to them. They view tattoos as a way of admiring their body and celebrating who they are.
  • Social Status: In these cultures, a tattoo can also represent your place and class in society.
  • Maori Warriors: In Aotearoa, Maori warriors would have tattoos to make them appear more threatening and to display their rank status or the tribe they were affiliated with. These designs were deeply tied to their other art forms, as the Maori would get their tattoo designs from woodcarving and other decorative arts.

Combating Stigmas and
Stereotypes

In modern Western society, tattoo culture has had to fight against negative stereotypes to be accepted as a legitimate art form.
